Nathalie El-Bazzal

PhD Candidate in Economics · University of Paris 8 · Research Affiliate at the Paris School of Economics

Growing up in an environment marked by recurrent geopolitical tensions and later working across the Middle East provided me with early exposure to the economic implications of political instability. Observing how conflict, sanctions, and geopolitical uncertainty disrupted trade, reshaped markets, and influenced firms' behavior motivated me to pursue research in international economics. I first explored these dynamics in my Master's theses, which examined the economic effects of the Syrian conflict. Over time, this work evolved into a broader research agenda on how geopolitical shocks propagate through international trade. As a PhD Candidate in Economics at the University of Paris 8 and a Research Affiliate at the Paris School of Economics, I investigate these issues with the support of the CEPR ReCIPE Big Research Grant, focusing on maritime trade, market integration, and global value chains.

Research

Working Paper

Trading Under Fire: Houthi Attacks and the Disruption of Global Shipping

I investigate how geopolitical shocks reshape global shipping networks and international trade, using the Houthi attacks in the Red Sea as a natural experiment. Drawing on high-frequency AIS and Kpler vessel-level data covering 2019–2025, I provide causal evidence on how the crisis affected vessel routing, shipping delays, and trade volumes.

Maritime Trade and Geopolitical Disruptions

Work in Progress

Geoeconomic Fragmentation and Market Integration during the Syrian Conflict

This project examines how territorial fragmentation, conflict intensity, and sanctions reshape local prices and market integration during the Syrian civil war. Using market-level price data for 77 products across 97 Syrian markets (2011–2025), combined with georeferenced conflict events, it identifies how fragmented governance and dependence on external trade generate heterogeneous market outcomes.

with Pierre-Louis Vézina (King's College London) and Jean-Charles Bricongne (Banque de France).
